Local pt-BR meeting notes on a 2 GB MX230 — benchmark report

Whisper large-v3-turbo / small (whisper.cpp, Vulkan) and Qwen3.5-2B / Qwen3-1.7B (llama.cpp b12527, Vulkan & CPU) · i7-10510U, 30 GB RAM, GeForce MX230 2 GB · measured 2026-11-19

Bottom line.
  • Transcription: stock large-v3-turbo q5_0 on the GPU is the pick for pt-BR — 1.9–2.5 % WER on the author’s recording (numbers spelled out) at ~0.36× real time in 824 MiB. small is 3× faster but makes ~5× more errors.
  • Swap (⇄) vs co-resident (⧉): switching turbo ↔ Qwen on the GPU costs only ~3 s. In the 30 s chunk experiment, swapping every chunk beat keeping both loaded (~37 s vs ~53 s of work per 30 s chunk on the 189 s clip): Qwen on CPU was 2–3× slower than on GPU under this machine's memory pressure. Neither layout keeps up live if it summarises after every chunk — transcription alone does (~17.7 s per 30 s chunk), the growing summary is what overflows.
  • Turbo and Qwen cannot both sit fully on the GPU (Qwen3.5-2B @8K = 1414 MiB + turbo 824 MiB > 2048 MiB → OOM). Splitting Qwen across GPU/CPU is the worst option (slows turbo ~35 %).
  • Summaries are the weak link: Qwen3.5-2B keeps names, dates and the two budget figures, but every final summary contained an invented or wrong fact (e.g. “prazo atrasado”, a wrong difference “26.250” instead of 32.500, an invented “Terça-feira” meeting date, “somando-se ao mapa”).
Legend used throughout: ⇄ SWAP models loaded/unloaded in turn on the GPU · ⧉ CO-RESIDENT two models loaded at the same time · ● SINGLE one model measured alone.

Cross-check — Yogurt’s own speed probe agrees on the ranking

Yogurt measured the same two models on this machine and picked small (its speech-speed.json). Its figures are higher than ours (it likely includes warm-up), but the ratio is the same ~3×.

{"model": "whisper-small-q5_1",
 "rtf": {"whisper-large-v3-turbo-q5_0": 0.601, "whisper-small-q5_1": 0.209}}

Gotcha found while measuring: in whisper.cpp the MX230 is -dev 0 (it counts discrete GPUs only). -dev 1 silently falls back to CPU — our first “GPU” run was actually CPU and was relabelled.

3 · Both models at once ⧉ CO-RESIDENT

Turbo + any Qwen fully on the GPU does not fit. What fits is either small + Qwen on GPU (bad pt-BR transcripts) or turbo on GPU with Qwen partly/fully on CPU. Keeping Qwen entirely on CPU barely slows turbo; splitting Qwen across GPU/CPU makes both compete and is the worst choice. But the CPU-side summary itself is slow — in the chunk experiment (§5) it lost to swapping Qwen onto the GPU.

5 · Process chunks — 30 s at a time

Audio is cut into 30 s chunks. For each chunk: transcribe with turbo, then ask Qwen3.5-2B to summarise the accumulated transcript so far (chunk 1 alone, chunk 1+2, …). After the last chunk a final summary of the full transcript runs on Qwen3.5-2B on the GPU (32K context). Two layouts:

  • ⇄ SWAP B11 — per chunk: load turbo (GPU) → transcribe → unload → load Qwen (GPU, 8K) → partial summary → unload.
  • ⧉ CO-RESIDENT B12 — turbo (GPU) and Qwen (CPU) stay loaded; same per-chunk work, no switching. The final summary still swaps to Qwen on GPU.

For live use, each chunk's work must finish in < 30 s or the backlog grows.

What the chunk runs show

  • Transcription per chunk is steady at ~17.7 s per 30 s (0.59×) in both layouts — so it is per-request overhead, not reload warm-up (turbo stayed loaded in ⧉ and was just as slow). Whole-file transcription ran at 0.36×; the exact cause of the per-request cost wasn't isolated. It still leaves ~12 s of slack per chunk.
  • Switching is cheap: ~3.5 s per chunk in total (load turbo ~0.7 s, load Qwen ~2.5 s, unloads <0.3 s), occasionally 5–11 s when RAM pressure evicted the model files from page cache (B10).
  • Qwen on CPU was the bottleneck in ⧉: 5–11 tok/s generation and 14–50 tok/s prompt reading while Whisper used the CPU threads too and free RAM was ~1 GB — summaries took 13–53 s vs 8–25 s on the GPU.
  • The accumulated summary grows with the transcript (prompt 139 → 641 tokens over 7 chunks), so later chunks cost more — summarising after every chunk can't hold the 30 s budget for long.
  • Final summaries hallucinate. Read them in the evidence blocks — the swap final for the author’s recording claims the contract “foi cancelado” and the deploy “foi atrasado”; the resident final computes a wrong difference and invents a meeting weekday; the MLS clip (a literary text, not a meeting) gets reframed as a meeting.

6 · What this implies for the app

Blue outline = runs on the MX230. Both models never sit on the GPU together; the swap costs ~3 s.

  • Live: keep turbo resident on the GPU and transcribe every 30 s chunk (0.59×, keeps up). Summarise on a slower cadence, not every chunk: pause turbo, swap to Qwen on the GPU (~3 s), summarise, swap back — the ~12 s/chunk slack absorbs the backlog. In this test, CPU-side summaries were slower than this swap.
  • Final: one swap at the end (~3 s) to Qwen3.5-2B on the GPU for the full-transcript summary.
  • Summary quality is the open risk, not speed — a stricter prompt or a bigger model on CPU (slower) are the levers to test next.

Caveats

  • One speaker reading clearly into a mic; real calls via system audio (compression, crosstalk) will score worse.
  • MLS pt is read audiobook speech, partly European Portuguese.
  • Model loads were mostly warm (page cache); a cold first load from disk is slower. RAM was heavily used (Yogurt held ~6.6 GB earlier), which also depresses CPU numbers.
  • WER uses a simple normaliser (lower-case, strip punctuation); one run per configuration, no repeats.
